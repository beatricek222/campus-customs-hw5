Reflection

My honest read on how the Campus Customs agent team did on tickets 101, 102 and 103, using the Ticket tabs (Expected vs Actual), the Cash tab, and the audit trail as evidence.

The short version

I went in expecting a team that was a little too eager to call everyone, and I came out pleasantly surprised: on all three tickets the Boss went to the right specialist first, never touched the money, never promised the customer anything the database could not back, and left every ticket at waiting_approval for a human, exactly as I predicted. What I did not love is the price of that care: 42 model requests, 50 tool calls and 213,778 tokens to resolve three tickets whose cash impact totalled $3,240, and a few small habits (status-flipping, repeated reads, one customer line that mentioned “approval”) that I would fix before trusting this with a real shop.

3 What could have been simpler as one agent with tools

If I were running Campus Customs for real, I would not use five agents for these tickets. Here is the evidence from our own runs.

The cost of having four agents re-read the same page

  • get_ticket_context was called 11 times across the three tickets (4 on 101, 3 on 102, 4 on 103) and each result was small (531 to 1,629 characters, per the audit trail). get_cash_outlook was called 6 times (twice per ticket).
  • Every agent starts a run with a long role prompt. The first request of each agent already carried about 3,200 to 4,100 input tokens (Boss 4,099; Accounting about 4,000; Inventory about 3,650; Customer Service about 3,700; Facilities 3,169) before it did any work. Since each later request re-sends that prompt, at least roughly 84% of ticket 101’s input tokens, 86% of ticket 102’s and 77% of ticket 103’s were just role prompts being re-read (my lower-bound estimate from the audit trail).
  • A single operator agent would carry one role prompt on each request instead of three or four different ones, and it would not repeat the same reads. I have not built or run that version, so I am not putting a token number on the saving; the measured part is how much of today’s bill is repeated prompt.

Why I say this

  • The specialists barely specialised. Facilities used two tools. Accounting’s write power (record_payment) was never used by an agent, because the real payments went through the dashboard’s approve route after I clicked; so Accounting in practice was a read-only analyst. Inventory and Accounting on 101 ran in parallel, but one agent can issue parallel tool calls too.
  • The cross-ticket insight did not need five agents. The valuable moment in ticket 102 (“the balance is already $2,560, so $160 remains”) came from one get_cash_outlook call and the Boss paying attention, not from the team structure.
  • Two agents wrote the same status field on 103 (Accounting set in_progress, then Customer Service set waiting_approval) while the Boss only commented on it in a note, which a single owner of the ticket would never do.

What I would still keep separate

  • A customer-facing writer. The Customer Service agent’s drafts for Tauhid Zaman and the Yale AI Club were clean on costs and balances, which is exactly why I would keep it separate. But I would change its data diet: it currently has quote_order, which returns unit cost and margin, so I would give it a customer-safe lookup (availability and list price only). Even with separation, the 103 draft mentioned that pricing “needs approval,” so a leak risk is real.
  • The approval gate. It is not an agent at all; it is the /approve-payment route and the record_payment rules (exact amount, no overdraft, human name). That is the right place for the most dangerous action, and it is already “one tool, no agent”.
  • The shape I would ship: one Operator agent with all the read tools plus notes and status, one Customer Writer with a restricted view, and the approval route. Inventory, Accounting and Facilities would become tool-backed skills inside the Operator, not separate loops. I only used five agents because this assignment, and its Expected versus Actual comparison, asked for a team; it was the right structure for learning and the wrong one for the invoice.

4 Three new problems this team could already solve

All three use only tools that exist today (get_cash_outlook, list_leases, quote_order, get_lease_due, list_stock_at_or_below, the approval routes) and data already in campus_customs_new.db.

Problem A · “Can we make the October 2 rent?”

The situation. The Cash tab ends at $160.00, and after ticket 102 lease 1’s next due date moved from 2026-09-02 to 2026-10-02. That is 32 days away on the shop’s calendar and $2,400 against $160, so the shop is $2,240 short unless sales arrive. This is a very real second-order effect of resolving the first three tickets.

How the team solves it. I would ask the Boss for a cash check. The Boss calls get_today and get_cash_outlook(32), which returns the balance ($160), the lease due on 2026-10-02 ($2,400), no open invoices (501 is paid), and a projected shortfall of $2,240. It asks Facilities to confirm the date with list_leases (days_until_due: 32) and Accounting to say what is payable and in what order. The Boss files the answer as a risk with exact figures and escalates to me with a recommendation to plan sales or a landlord conversation. We already saw Accounting do a version of this unprompted on ticket 103 (“$2,504 shortfall including rent and the restock”).

Where a human stays in: deciding what to do about a $2,240 gap. The agents can see it early; they cannot create the cash.

Problem B · A second student group wants 30 Yale Caps at a discount

The situation. A different group asks for 30 CC-HAT-BLUE (Yale Cap) at a bulk price. The inventory table has 40 caps (one size, Aisle C), unit cost $6, list $22. Unlike the hoodie request on ticket 103, this one can be filled entirely from stock. (The 40 caps, $6 cost and $22 list are from the database; the 30-cap request and the $18 asking price are hypothetical inputs I chose for the example.)

How the team solves it. The Boss starts with Inventory, as it did on 103. quote_order(CC-HAT-BLUE, OS, 30, 18) returns can_fill_from_stock: true, shortfall 0, list total $660, cost $180, margin $480 at list and $360 at $18 (about 18.2% off list), and below_cost: false; the floor is $6. Customer Service drafts a holding reply (no discount hinted). The ticket goes to waiting_approval, I accept or change the price from the board, and after the caps are physically handed over Inventory records adjust_inventory(-30), leaving 10. Accounting is not needed, because no cash leaves, which is the contrast with ticket 103 where a reorder pulled it in.

Where a human stays in: choosing the price. As ticket 103 showed ($52 was the model’s judgment), the database has no discount rule, so the agents can bound the decision (never below $6) but not make it.

Problem C · A suspicious or mismatched landlord email

The situation. A message arrives that looks like ticket 102 but says rent is $2,800 due tomorrow and asks us to pay a “new account”. Lease 1 says Elm City Properties, $2,400. (The $2,800 and the “new account” are hypothetical; the $2,400 is from lease 1.)

How the team solves it. Facilities runs get_ticket_context and get_lease_due(1) and compares each claim: the amount ($2,800 vs $2,400), the date, and the request to change payee details. Its prompt tells it to flag a payee change as a red flag instead of following it. The Boss sets the ticket to escalated. Even if an agent were fooled, record_payment only accepts an amount equal to the lease’s monthly_rent and a named human approver, so a $2,800 payment is impossible. This is the same “verify the claim against the lease” move that worked cleanly on ticket 102, now with a mismatch to catch.

Where a human stays in: calling the landlord through a trusted channel.

5 Three new problems this team could not solve yet, and what I would build

Problem D · Actually placing the restock (the 12 hoodies and the S tee)

Why the current system fails. On ticket 103 the team could say we are 12 hoodies short and that a restock is “about $264 at unit cost”, but that number was derived from pricing.unit_cost; there is no vendor price. The vendors table has no SKU mapping (Bulldog Print Co was an inference from the specialty text “apparel reprint”), and the database has no purchase-order table, so no agent can request a quote, place an order or record one. On ticket 101, paying invoice 501 ($840) was a bet that the reprint would follow; nothing in the system can confirm production or delivery, and the 2026-09-05 date is only today plus 5 lead days.

What I would build.

  • New tables: vendor_catalog (vendor_id, sku, quoted_unit_cost, min_qty, lead_days), purchase_orders and po_lines (status: draft, approved, sent, received).
  • New MCP tools: get_vendor_quote(sku, qty), draft_purchase_order, submit_purchase_order (gated by a human approver exactly like record_payment), get_order_status, and receive_shipment (which calls adjust_inventory).
  • New agent: a Procurement agent that owns vendors and purchase orders. It would work with Inventory (shortfall and receiving) and Accounting (does the quote fit in cash, alongside the Oct 2 rent?), and it would replace Inventory’s guesswork with a quote.
  • Dashboard: a second approval card for a purchase order, next to the payment card.

Problem E · Closing the loop with customers (sending, reserving, following up)

Why the current system fails. The replies to Tauhid Zaman (101) and the Yale AI Club (103) are drafts: there is no send tool, so a person must copy and send them. There is no order or customer table, only tickets, so nothing reserves the 8 hoodies we do have for the club or records that Tauhid is waiting for an S tee. And a resolved ticket is a dead end: when the reprint arrives, nothing wakes the team to tell him. Refunds and pickups do not exist at all.

What I would build.

  • New tables: customers, orders and order_lines, reservations, and an outbox of messages.
  • New MCP tools: reserve_stock(sku, size, qty, order_id), create_order, queue_customer_message (a draft in the outbox that a human approves before it is sent), send_approved_message, mark_fulfilled, refund_payment (approval-gated), and a list_waiting_customers(sku, size) lookup.
  • New agents/triggers: a Fulfillment agent that reserves, ships and refunds, and a Follow-up trigger that fires on receive_shipment so Customer Service can message everyone waiting on that SKU (for example Tauhid Zaman for CC-TEE-WHITE size S).
  • Privacy fix: a customer-safe view tool (availability and list price only) so the writer can never see cost or margin, which would also close the “needs approval” leak I noticed on 103.

Problem F · Forecasting cash and managing timing (the $160 problem)

Why the current system fails. The Cash tab ends with $160.00 and a $2,400 rent due on 2026-10-02. The agents can compute the $2,240 shortfall, but nothing in the database records sales, so they cannot say whether weekly revenue will close the gap. There is one account (checking) and no way to move money between accounts, to schedule a payment, or to ask a landlord for more time. And nothing runs by itself: invoice 501 sat 3 days overdue before anyone clicked Run, because the team only works when I start it from the board.

What I would build.

  • New tables: sales (date, sku, size, qty, price) and scheduled_payments, plus more rows in cash_accounts (savings, a card) so transfers mean something.
  • New MCP tools: get_sales_summary(days), forecast_cash(days_ahead) (balance plus expected sales minus obligations, shown with a range, not a point), schedule_payment and transfer_funds (both approval-gated), list_due_soon(days), and a draft_landlord_request that queues an extension email for a human to send.
  • New agent: a Treasury agent that owns the forecast and the payment calendar, plus a daily scheduler that runs it each morning and posts “what is due or overdue” to the board. Had this existed, the overdue $840 invoice and the $2,240 October gap would have shown up as alerts instead of surprises.

If I could build only one next: Problem D. Tickets 101 and 103 both ended by saying “we need to restock but cannot confirm how.” Everything else the team did was solid; the purchase side is the first place it hits the edge of its tools.
